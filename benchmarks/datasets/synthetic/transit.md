# Harbor City orders two battery electric ferries

Harbor City Transit ordered two battery electric ferries from Northshore Marine for $38 million. Each vessel will carry 300 passengers between downtown and Gull Island.

The ferries will replace two diesel boats built in the 1980s. Transit officials expect the switch to cut ferry fuel costs by about 70 percent.

Delivery of the first vessel is planned for late 2027.

# Ferry charging dock construction starts at Pier 9

Crews began building the ferry charging dock at Pier 9 on Wednesday, the first step toward the city's "zero emission" ferry route. The dock will charge a ferry in about 15 minutes.

Harbor Power is adding a substation near the pier to supply the chargers. The work will close part of the pier walkway until spring.

The dock is funded by a federal port grant.

# Electric buses will run on routes 4 and 11 from April

Harbor City Transit will move routes 4 and 11 to electric buses starting April 6. The two routes carry about 9,000 riders a day.

Schedules will not change, but some stops will move to make room for charging at the route ends. Riders can see the new stop locations on the transit website.

More routes will switch as buses arrive.

# Council approves $48 million for transit electrification

The Harbor City council approved $48 million for ferry and bus electrification over the next four years. The money covers vessels, buses, and chargers.

About half the total comes from federal grants. The rest comes from the city's transit bond.

The council will review spending every six months.

# Bus fares will rise by 25 cents in July

Harbor City bus fares will rise by 25 cents on July 1, the first increase in six years. A single ride will cost $2.75.

Monthly passes will rise by $5. Reduced fares for seniors and students will not change.

Transit officials said rising labor costs drove the increase.

# A short history of Harbor City's wooden ferries

Harbor City's first ferries were wooden steamers that crossed to Gull Island in the 1890s. The last wooden ferry retired in 1951.

The maritime museum keeps the steamer Gull Queen on display at Pier 2. Volunteers lead tours on weekends.

The museum plans an exhibit on the new electric ferries next year.

# Scooter share expands to three new neighborhoods

The scooter share program will expand to Eastgate, Millbrook, and the Old Port next month. The operator is adding 600 scooters.

Riders pay a dollar to unlock a scooter and 30 cents a minute. Parking corrals will be painted near major intersections.

The city will review the program after one year.

# First electric ferry delivery moves up to September 2027

Northshore Marine will deliver Harbor City's first battery electric ferry in September 2027, three months earlier than planned. The second vessel follows in early 2028.

The ferries will carry 300 passengers each between downtown and Gull Island. Crews will train on the first vessel before it enters service.

Transit officials said the earlier date will cut diesel use sooner.

# Bus depot gets 30 new chargers for electric buses

Harbor City Transit installed 30 chargers at the Westside bus depot to serve the electric buses on routes 4 and 11. The chargers can refill a bus overnight.

The depot will add 20 more chargers next year as more electric buses arrive.

The project was finished two weeks ahead of schedule.

# Weekly fare cap proposed for frequent bus and ferry riders

Harbor City Transit proposed a weekly fare cap so riders never pay more than $25 in a week. Riders would need a reloadable card to qualify.

The transit board will vote on the proposal next month. A public hearing is set for the 18th.

The cap would cover both buses and ferries.

# Ferry crews begin battery safety training

Harbor City ferry crews began battery safety training this week ahead of the first electric ferry delivery. The course covers fire response and charging procedures.

Every captain and deckhand must finish the course before working on the new vessels.

The training is run by the state maritime academy.
